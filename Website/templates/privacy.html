Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026

In short

Card Barcodes collects no personal data. I have no servers, no analytics and no advertising, so I never see your cards, your location or how you use the app.

Only two things leave your device without you sending them, and both go to Apple, not to me: what Apple Maps needs to find and name the places you add, and your cards, if you sync them through iCloud.

Data I collect

None. Nothing in the app sends your data to me, and it has no third-party code that collects information.

What leaves your device

  • Apple Maps, when you add or change a place. As you type in the search field, the app sends what you have typed, with the area the map shows, to Apple Maps for suggestions, and then for the place you choose. To name a place, it sends Apple Maps the position of the pin once you stop moving the map, which is where you are when the map opens on your location. The map loads the area on screen from Apple too. Apple handles these requests under its own privacy policy, and they never reach me.
  • iCloud, if you are signed in. Your cards sync to your other devices through your own iCloud account, as below.
  • Nothing else. The app makes no other requests over the internet. It has no analytics, no adverts and no crash reporting of its own.

What you choose to send

  • Sharing a card. Share Link makes a link with the whole card in it, after the “#”: its name, code, barcode type, colour, icon and places, and whether it reminds you when you arrive. Browsers never send that part of a link to a website, so opening it on cards.serby.net does not send the card to me. Share Image makes a picture of the card. Either goes only to whoever you send it to, through the app you choose.
  • Exporting. Export as JSON writes all your cards to a file, only when you ask.
  • Opening a place in Maps. Tapping one of a card's places opens it in the Maps app, with its name and position.

Where your cards are stored

Your cards are stored on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud, they also sync through your own iCloud account to your other devices, using Apple's CloudKit private database. That data belongs to your Apple Account: Apple stores it under its own privacy policy, and I cannot see or access it.

  • A card holds its name, its code and barcode type, its colour and icon, the places you add to it with their names, positions and sizes, whether it reminds you when you arrive, where it sits in your list, and when you added it and last opened it
  • The Home Screen widget keeps a copy of your cards on your device, which only the app and the widget can read
  • Your device's security (Face ID, Touch ID, passcode) protects them
  • Deleting a card deletes it on all your devices

Location

Location is optional. If you allow it, the app and its widget use your location on your device to:

  • Show how far away each card's places are
  • Pick the nearest card for the Home Screen widget
  • Open the map at where you are when you add a place
  • Choose which of your cards' places iOS watches for arrival notifications

Your location stays on your device, except that Apple Maps is asked to name the spot under the map's pin when you add or change a place, as above. The places you add to a card are stored and synced with the card.

iOS watches for you reaching a card's places on your device and shows the notification, with no server involved. Arrival notifications can be turned off in the app's Settings.

Camera and photos

The app uses the camera only to scan barcodes while you are adding a card. Camera images are read on your device and are never recorded, stored or sent anywhere.

Choose Photo and Choose File read a barcode from the one picture or file you pick, on your device. When you save a shared card's picture to Photos, iOS asks whether the app may add photos; it never asks to see them.

Notifications

Notifications are created on your device. No notification service of mine is involved.

Ratings

Once you have opened cards on three different days in a version, the app may ask for a rating with Apple's own prompt. It counts those days on your device only, and Apple decides whether the prompt appears.

No analytics or advertising

The app has no analytics and no adverts, and nothing in it tracks you. It never reports which features you use or what cards you keep, and I will never sell data because I have none to sell.

Apple tells me how many times the app is downloaded. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, Apple also sends me crash reports and usage figures that do not identify you.
