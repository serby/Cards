Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

In short

Card Barcodes never sends me your cards, your places, where you are or anything personal about you. I have no servers of my own and no advertising, and nothing in the app tracks you.

Two things help me fix and improve the app, and each is one switch in the app's Settings, under Privacy, to turn off at any time: crash reports and performance data, sent to Sentry, on until you turn it off, and counts of which features are used, sent to Google Analytics, off until you turn it on. Nothing is sent while a switch is off. See Diagnostics and usage data.

Only two things leave your devices without you sending them, and both go to Apple, not to me: what Apple Maps needs to find and name the places you add, and your cards, if you sync them through iCloud. Your iPhone also gives your Apple Watch, if the app is on it, a copy of your cards, from one of your devices to the other.

This website, unlike the app, can count visits with Google Analytics, but only if you allow it (see This website).

Data I collect

No personal data, and none of your cards. I never receive a card, a code, a barcode, a note, a place, your location, your name, your email or your IP address. With your agreement, and only then, I receive the crash reports and usage counts below, which say how the app behaves, not who you are or what you keep in it.

What leaves your device

  • Apple Maps, when you add or change a place. As you type in the search field, the app sends what you have typed, with the area the map shows, to Apple Maps for suggestions, and then for the place you choose. To name a place, it sends Apple Maps the position of the pin once you stop moving the map, which is where you are when the map opens on your location. The map loads the area on screen from Apple too. Apple handles these requests under its own privacy policy, and they never reach me.
  • iCloud, if you are signed in and iCloud Sync is on. Your cards sync to your other devices through your own iCloud account, as below.
  • Your Apple Watch, if the app is on it. Your iPhone sends the Apple Watch paired with it a copy of your cards, without their notes, through Apple's own connection between the two, whether or not iCloud Sync is on. It never reaches me.
  • Sentry, while Share Diagnostics is on. Crash reports and performance data, as below.
  • Google Analytics, once you turn Share Usage Data on. Counts of which features are used, as below.
  • Nothing else. The app makes no other requests over the internet, and it has no adverts.

What you choose to send

  • Sharing a card. Share Link makes a link with the whole card in it, after the “#”: its name, code, barcode type, colour, icon and places, and whether it reminds you when you arrive. Browsers never send that part of a link to a website, so opening it on cards.serby.net does not send the card to me. Share Image makes a picture of the card. Either goes only to whoever you send it to, through the app you choose.
  • Exporting. Export as JSON writes all your cards to a file, only when you ask.
  • Opening a place in a maps app. Tapping one of a card's places opens it in the maps app chosen in Settings > Maps. Apple Maps, the default, gets its name and position. Google Maps gets its position, in its app or, if that isn't installed, on its website, under Google's own privacy policy.

Where your cards are stored

Your cards are stored on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud, they also sync through your own iCloud account to your other devices, using Apple's CloudKit private database. That data belongs to your Apple Account: Apple stores it under its own privacy policy, and I cannot see or access it.

To keep your cards on your device only, turn off iCloud Sync in the app's Settings. The cards already in iCloud stay there until you choose Delete Cards from iCloud, which deletes them from iCloud and keeps them on your device. Another device that still has iCloud Sync on will upload its cards again.

  • A card holds its name, its code and barcode type, its colour and icon, the places you add to it with their names, positions and sizes, whether it reminds you when you arrive, where it sits in your list, and when you added it and last opened it
  • The Home Screen widget keeps a copy of your cards on your device, which only the app and the widget can read
  • The Apple Watch app keeps the copy your iPhone sends it on your watch, where only the watch app can read it
  • Your device's security (Face ID, Touch ID, passcode) protects them
  • Deleting a card deletes it on all your devices

Location

Location is optional. If you allow it, the app, its widget and its Apple Watch app use your location on your device to:

  • Show how far away each card's places are
  • Pick the nearest card for the Home Screen widget
  • Open the card for the place you are at on your Apple Watch
  • Open the map at where you are when you add a place
  • Choose which of your cards' places iOS watches for arrival notifications

Your location stays on your device, except that Apple Maps is asked to name the spot under the map's pin when you add or change a place, as above. The places you add to a card are stored and synced with the card.

iOS watches for you reaching a card's places on your device and shows the notification, with no server involved. Arrival notifications can be turned off in the app's Settings.

Camera and photos

The app uses the camera only to scan barcodes while you are adding a card. Camera images are read on your device and are never recorded, stored or sent anywhere.

Choose Photo and Choose File read a barcode from the one picture or file you pick, on your device. When you save a shared card's picture to Photos, iOS asks whether the app may add photos; it never asks to see them.

Notifications

Notifications are created on your device. No notification service of mine is involved.

Ratings

Once you have opened cards on three different days in a version, the app may ask for a rating with Apple's own prompt. It counts those days on your device only, and Apple decides whether the prompt appears.

This website

cards.serby.net asks before it counts your visit. If you choose Allow, it loads Google Tag Manager and Google Analytics, which set cookies in your browser and send Google the pages you open, the site that sent you, your browser and device type, and your approximate location from your IP address. I see these as totals, to learn which pages help people, never as anything that names you. Google handles the data under its own privacy policy, at policies.google.com.

If you choose No thanks, or choose nothing, no Google script loads and no cookie is set. The site remembers your choice in your browser, and Cookie Settings, at the foot of every page, lets you change it at any time; choosing No thanks then also removes Google Analytics' cookies.

The share link page, add.html, never loads analytics, so a shared card is never counted. Your choice here has nothing to do with the app, which counts nothing unless you turn on Share Usage Data in its own Settings.

Diagnostics and usage data

Neither ever includes your cards or anything personal, and each is one switch to turn off. Both switches are in the app's Settings, under Privacy. Turning one off stops it at once, and nothing is sent while it is off.

Share Diagnostics, on until you turn it off, sends crash reports and performance data to Sentry (sentry.io), so I can find and fix problems: what the app was doing when it crashed or froze, how long things took, such as drawing a barcode or opening the app, short notes the app writes about what it did, such as "a card was saved", and the app's version, iOS's version, the device model and its free memory and storage. With it off, the app never starts Sentry.

Share Usage Data, off until you turn it on, counts which parts of the app are used with Google Analytics (Google's Firebase), so I can decide what to build next: a screen opened, a card added and how (scanned, typed, from a photo or from a link), a barcode shown, a card shared, cards exported or imported, a setting changed, the widget or the watch app used. Turning it off also deletes the identifier Google Analytics kept for the app on your device.

Neither sends a card's name, code, barcode, notes, colour, icon or places, or where you are, and neither takes a picture of the screen. Neither sends your name, your email, your Apple Account or the advertising identifier, so iOS never asks to let the app track you. Each keeps its own random identifier for the app on your device, to count one install once; it says nothing about who you are, and deleting the app removes it. Sentry is set never to store the IP address a report comes from, and Google Analytics does not store IP addresses. Sentry keeps reports for 90 days and Google Analytics keeps its events for 2 months, and neither is used for advertising or shared with anyone for it. Sentry and Google handle the data under their own privacy policies, at sentry.io/privacy and policies.google.com.

Apple tells me how many times the app is downloaded. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, Apple also sends me crash reports and usage figures that do not identify you. The app's own switches do not change that setting, which only you can change in iOS's Settings.

I will never sell data, and I will never add adverts.
